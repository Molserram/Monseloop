## Platform

web

## Users
Primary: hiring managers and recruiters evaluating Monserrat Molina Ramirez for an in-house visual/product design role. They land on the homepage, skim the hero and case studies, and use the three case studies as work-history proof points — evidence of how she operates inside a company over time, not just what she can produce as a one-off.

Secondary: freelance or agency clients considering commissioning branding, website, or presentation design work directly (evidenced by the Behance-linked "More work" grid — TEDx Caleidoscopio, Cachito Mío, Design Thinking Template, etc.).

## Product Purpose
A visual designer's portfolio site. It exists to get hiring managers and prospective clients to two outcomes: reach out via email, or click through to Behance/case studies for deeper proof. Success is a contact/inquiry, not just admiration of the visuals.

## Positioning
Monserrat is a systems thinker who scales ad hoc, inconsistent creative work into repeatable design systems — and that skill transfers across eras of tooling. All three homepage case studies prove the same underlying mechanism from a different angle:
- **Tomorrow Problem** — turning a one-off AI feature launch into a reusable multi-format visual language.
- **Growth Process Overhaul** — turning an orphaned website with no process into a scalable publishing pipeline.
- **Evolution of Templates** — turning template design for manual customization into design that survives being generated by an AI (Storyblocks → Open Canvas → AI generation → prompting with Claude).

The through-line a competitor portfolio could not truthfully copy: she has iterated the same "build the system, not just the artifact" instinct across three real shifts in how the underlying product generates design (manual → templated → AI-assisted), inside one long-tenure role — not scattered one-off freelance gigs.

## Operating Context
- Nine years at Prezi as a remote visual/product designer — this is in-house employment history, not a series of external client engagements, even though the case-study meta rows label the field "Client." All three published case studies come from that one tenure.
- The homepage "More work" grid links out to Behance for additional, non-case-study projects: TEDx Caleidoscopio (real conference client), Cachito Mío (an ongoing real branding business), plus additional Prezi-affiliated presentation/template work.
- Visitors move: homepage hero → Selected Work (3 case studies) → More Work (Behance) → About → Contact. Case study pages are standalone (own back-to-work link, own next-case-study chain) so they must read coherently if landed on directly from a shared link.

## Capabilities and Constraints
- Static HTML/CSS/JS, no build step, no framework, no package.json. Each page is self-contained (CSS and JS inlined in `<style>`/`<script>` tags) by deliberate choice — `assets/style.css` and `assets/site.js` are reference copies only, kept in sync but not linked, because external `<link>`/`<script>` references broke under `file://` testing.
- No backend, no CMS, no forms — contact is a `mailto:` link and static Behance/social links.
- Video assets play inline via `<video autoplay muted loop playsinline>`; several case-study visuals are horizontally scrolling image carousels (custom lightweight scroll-snap implementation, no library).
- Undecided: whether a contact form, analytics, or a CMS-driven case-study list will ever be added. Do not assume any of these exist.

## Product Principles
1. Every surface should ultimately point toward "does this prove she can run a design system inside a real company over time" — not just "does this look good."
2. Case studies are read as work-history evidence first, portfolio pieces second; process and reasoning matter as much as the final visual.
3. Treat the continuous-stroke mark, the type system, and the measured first-person voice as fixed brand identity, not open variables — changes here should be deliberate, not incidental to a feature request.
4. Keep the site technically honest: no invented forms, integrations, or content the static/no-backend architecture doesn't actually support.
5. Case-study pages must stand alone since they're shareable links, not just steps in a homepage scroll.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
No project-specific accessibility requirement has been established beyond standard semantic HTML, keyboard navigation, and `prefers-reduced-motion` support already present in the codebase.
